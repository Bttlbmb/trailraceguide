Pace by leg

What it shows

Leg pace divided by whole-race pace, averaged over runners with complete checkpoint times. 1.10× means 10% slower.

Reading it

The colour key gives the thresholds; the printed factor is the exact value.

CP3 to CP4 is the slowest leg: at 1.58× it took 58% more time per km than the race average. CP2 to CP3 is the fastest at 0.77×.

Keep in mind

Pace, not effort: stops count, and the figure resolves only between checkpoints.

Missing times are not estimated. Even when these minimums are met, the timed runners may not represent all finishers.

Sampling locations

For each edition, the three locations are the lowest and highest points of the smoothed course profile and a point halfway between them in elevation. If the course crosses that middle elevation more than once, use the crossing closest to halfway along the course by distance. All three locations are sampled for the full race window; the estimates do not describe the weather experienced by an individual runner.

Where the data comes from

Trail Race Guide is independent of the race organiser and the data providers. Records from past races can be incomplete or contain errors. The official race website lists the current route, cut-offs and rules.

Course

Distance and climb

The distance shown here follows the organiser’s GPX track. Distance is measured horizontally. Elevation is sampled every 20 m and smoothed over ±60 m before the climbs are added together. The organiser’s 2026 figures appear alongside these measurements. Checkpoint locations and reported distances come from the timing provider’s list.

Climb comparison

Other courses’ published climb figures come from Maarten Poirot’s UTMB Index dataset. Each course counts once, using its latest edition (2017–2024) with at least 100 participants. The distance range starts at ±2% and widens until 100 courses qualify.

Terrain picture

Terrain heights come from Copernicus GLO-30. The red line follows the course track. Use the official course map for navigation. ESA WorldCover 2021 identifies built-up areas in brown and water in blue.
