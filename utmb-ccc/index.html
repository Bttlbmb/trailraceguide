Pace by leg

What it shows

Leg pace divided by whole-race pace, averaged over runners with complete checkpoint times. 1.10× means 10% slower.

Reading it

The colour key gives the thresholds; the printed factor is the exact value.

CP8 to CP9 is the slowest leg: at 1.90× it took 90% more time per km than the race average. CP1 to CP2 is the fastest at 0.62×.

Keep in mind

Pace, not effort: stops count, and the figure resolves only between checkpoints.

1,603 of 1,641 (97.7%) finishers had complete checkpoint times in 2025.

  • 60–80% 323 of 328 (98.5%)

Each of the five finish-rank groups must have at least 10 runners with valid times at every checkpoint, including start and finish, and those runners must cover at least 50% of their group. Missing times are not estimated. Even when these minimums are met, the timed runners may not represent all finishers.

Vallorcine Entrée and Vallorcine Sortie are at the same station. Time between visits counts towards the next leg.

Air and feels-like ranges, peak gust and highest precipitation across the reference sites. Feels-like accounts for wind, humidity and sunlight. The last row shows the range across editions.

Reading it

Both bars share one scale, low (blue) to high (orange). Raindrops: solid from 5 mm, pale from 1 mm, outlined below.

In 2025 the lowest estimated feels-like temperature (-3 °C) was 3° below the lowest estimated air temperature (0 °C). The two minima may occur at different locations or times.

Keep in mind

Estimates cover the listed sites and hours; the model grid (about 25 km) smooths valley and ridge conditions.

Sampling locations

For each edition, the three locations are the lowest and highest points of the smoothed course profile and a point halfway between them in elevation. If the course crosses that middle elevation more than once, use the crossing closest to halfway along the course by distance. All three locations are sampled for the full race window; the estimates do not describe the weather experienced by an individual runner.

Where the data comes from

Trail Race Guide is independent of the race organiser and the data providers. Records from past races can be incomplete or contain errors. The official race website lists the current route, cut-offs and rules.

Course

Distance and climb

The distance shown here follows the organiser’s GPX track. Distance is measured horizontally. Elevation is sampled every 20 m and smoothed over ±60 m before the climbs are added together. The organiser’s 2025 figures appear alongside these measurements. Checkpoint locations and reported distances come from the timing provider’s list.

Climb comparison

Other courses’ published climb figures come from Maarten Poirot’s UTMB Index dataset. Each course counts once, using its latest edition (2016–2024) with at least 100 participants. The distance range starts at ±2% and widens until 100 courses qualify.

Terrain picture

Terrain heights come from Copernicus GLO-30. The red line follows the course track. Use the official course map for navigation. ESA WorldCover 2021 identifies built-up areas in brown and water in blue.

Finish times

LiveTrail results by year · 2025 · 2024 · 2023

Pace between checkpoints

The 2025 LiveTrail checkpoint times show how each runner’s pace on a section compares with their average pace for the whole race. Both figures include time spent at stops.

Similar races

Similar races fall within ±5% of this course’s measured distance and ±15% of its climb, using published figures from Maarten Poirot’s UTMB Index dataset (2016–2024). The wider climb band allows for uncertain measurement methods. Finish comparisons exclude conflicting finisher counts. The bars use matched DUV results. Every edition counts equally. Poirot supplies the overall averages. Matches check finish clocks, year, distance, field size and average time. Races with full results: 71 for all finishers, 31 for women, 71 for men.

Daylight

Finish crossings come from the 2025 LiveTrail timing archive. The line marks the scheduled start. Sunrise and sunset estimates apply to the start location. Parts of the course can fall into mountain shade earlier.

Race-day weather

The weather figures summarise hourly ERA5 estimates from Open-Meteo at the lowest, middle-elevation and highest course points during race hours.

What it shows

Air and feels-like ranges, peak gust and highest precipitation across the reference sites. Feels-like accounts for wind, humidity and sunlight.

Reading it

Both bars share one scale, low (blue) to high (orange). Raindrops: solid from 5 mm, pale from 1 mm, outlined below.

In 2025 the lowest estimated feels-like temperature (-3 °C) was 3° below the lowest estimated air temperature (0 °C). The two minima may occur at different locations or times.

Keep in mind

Estimates cover the listed sites and hours; the model grid (about 25 km) smooths valley and ridge conditions.

Sampling locations

For each edition, the three locations are the lowest and highest points of the smoothed course profile and a point halfway between them in elevation. If the course crosses that middle elevation more than once, use the crossing closest to halfway along the course by distance. All three locations are sampled for the full race window; the estimates do not describe the weather experienced by an individual runner.

Where the data comes from

Trail Race Guide is independent of the race organiser and the data providers. Records from past races can be incomplete or contain errors. The official race website lists the current route, cut-offs and rules.

Distance and climb

The distance shown here follows the organiser’s GPX track. Distance is measured horizontally. Elevation is sampled every 20 m and smoothed over ±60 m before the climbs are added together. The organiser’s 2025 figures appear alongside these measurements. Checkpoint locations and reported distances come from the timing provider’s list.

Climb comparison

Other courses’ published climb figures come from Maarten Poirot’s UTMB Index dataset. Each course counts once, using its latest edition (2016–2024) with at least 100 participants. The distance range starts at ±2% and widens until 100 courses qualify.

Terrain picture

Terrain heights come from Copernicus GLO-30. The red line follows the course track. Use the official course map for navigation. ESA WorldCover 2021 identifies built-up areas in brown and water in blue.

Pace between checkpoints

The 2025 LiveTrail checkpoint times show how each runner’s pace on a section compares with their average pace for the whole race. Both figures include time spent at stops.

Similar races

Similar races fall within ±5% of this course’s measured distance and ±15% of its climb, using published figures from Maarten Poirot’s UTMB Index dataset (2016–2024). The wider climb band allows for uncertain measurement methods. Finish comparisons exclude conflicting finisher counts. The bars use matched DUV results. Every edition counts equally. Poirot supplies the overall averages. Matches check finish clocks, year, distance, field size and average time. Races with full results: 71 for all finishers, 31 for women, 71 for men.

Daylight

Finish crossings come from the 2025 LiveTrail timing archive. The line marks the scheduled start. Sunrise and sunset estimates apply to the start location. Parts of the course can fall into mountain shade earlier.

Race-day weather

The weather figures summarise hourly ERA5 estimates from Open-Meteo at the lowest, middle-elevation and highest course points during race hours.
